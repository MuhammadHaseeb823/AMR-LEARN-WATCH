Page 01 · Rule-based microbiology logic

Laboratory Intelligence

Versioned rule interpretation of AST results. The case generator is for practice and demonstration.

This module never predicts susceptibility from an image or an organism's identity alone. Real antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST) requires physically exposing a grown isolate to each antimicrobial (disk diffusion or broth microdilution) — this tool interprets S/I/R results you already have, using deterministic logic loosely informed by EUCAST expert-rule concepts. It also includes an illustrated identification exercise, because in a real workflow the organism is identified from culture before susceptibility is ever tested.

How this actually works — no shortcuts. Stage A below is an interactive diagram-based exercise (drawn illustrations, not photographs, and not automated image recognition) that mirrors real presumptive-identification reasoning: Gram stain → culture appearance → confirmatory biochemical test → presumptive organism. Stage B then asks you for real (or practice) AST results and interprets the pattern. Nothing here diagnoses a patient or replaces a biomedical scientist.

Surveillance Intelligence

Statistical detection of resistance trends. The series generator is for practice and demonstration.

Simulates a 24-month resistance-proportion series and runs it through three named, classical statistical detectors side by side — a moving average, EWMA, and CUSUM — rather than one arbitrary threshold. None of these are trained machine-learning models; they are transparent, reproducible formulas, which is a deliberate and more defensible design choice for a screening tool.

Validation

Generates a batch of seeded synthetic cases and computes real accuracy/sensitivity/false-alarm metrics against them — the same honest pattern used in the full research build, just computed on the spot instead of pre-baked. Read the caveat below before citing any number.

What these numbers do and don't mean. The laboratory "ground truth" here is whatever the same deterministic rule engine outputs for that AST pattern — there is no independent wet-lab oracle in a browser. Near-100% laboratory accuracy is a self-consistency check, not evidence of real diagnostic performance. The early-warning comparison is more informative: sensitivity, false-alarm rate and detection delay genuinely differ between the three statistical detectors on the same generated data.

About this system

        

          What is actually AI here, and what isn't?
          

            Rule-derived Laboratory identification and AST interpretation — fixed decision logic, no learning, fully reproducible.

            Statistical Early-warning detection (moving average / EWMA / CUSUM) and stewardship trend audit — classical formulas, not trained models.

            Generative (optional) Plain-language rewriting of a rule-derived finding, only if you supply an API key — it explains, it does not decide.

            Uncertain Shown whenever evidence is missing, contradictory, or outside this tool's organism list, instead of guessing.
          

          
No component here is a trained, validated machine-learning classifier. Calling the whole system "AI-powered" without that distinction would overstate what it does — so this page states it plainly instead.

        

        

          Data, ethics & reproducibility
          
All data is synthetic, generated in your browser, and nothing is transmitted anywhere unless you explicitly enable the optional generative layer. Session data (tallies, findings, audit trail) is saved only to this browser's local storage on this device — not to any server, not synced across devices, and visible to no one else. No patients, biological specimens or human participants are involved, so this computational demonstration would not typically require research ethics approval — but that determination should still be confirmed with your institution before any results are used in a publication. Random generation is seeded: setting the session seed (Evidence page) and repeating the same actions in the same order reproduces identical practice cases, series and results. Generative explanations and server data are not reproducible by seed.

        

        

          Current limitations / roadmap
          
This single-file version does not include: a trained ML model (the optional image analysis on the Laboratory page is classical computer vision — thresholding and blob counting — not a learned model), a locked validation dataset with ground-truth labels, sensitivity/specificity/F1 benchmarking, multi-user accounts, or a persistent server-side database. Those need a real backend and are a natural next phase, not something a static HTML file run in one browser can honestly claim to do.

Getting started


What AMR-WATCH is


AMR-WATCH is the surveillance edition of a teaching and research app about antimicrobial resistance (AMR). It shows how an infection prevention team might look at resistance trends, how classical statistical detectors flag a rise, and how a flag is investigated and reported. It is a demonstration, not a clinical tool.


Synthetic data only. Every isolate count, series and case in the app is generated in your browser from a numbered seed. There are no real patients. The banner at the top of every page says: teaching and research demonstration, not a medical device, not validated for clinical use, not for patient care, do not enter real patient data. The link in that banner opens Reliability, which lists what the system does and does not claim.


How it runs


The whole app is one HTML file that runs in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you sign in to an optional server (the "Server connection" box on Overview). Without a server the app says "browser demo mode". Records you keep (investigations, session tallies, audit trail) are saved in this browser on this device only.


Navigation


The page bar lists ten pages: Overview, Investigations, Laboratory, Surveillance, Trends, Stewardship, Validation, Reliability, Evidence and Reports. Opening the app with no page chosen takes you to Trends. Some pages have a row of sub-tabs under the heading (for example Overview: Now, Evidence, Ask, System). Use the mouse, or the left and right arrow keys, Home and End once a tab has focus. Each page and sub-tab has a web address of the form #/page or #/page/subtab, so you can bookmark it.


Role view selector


The "Role view" drop-down in the header has five options: All views, Laboratory, Infection prevention, Stewardship and Executive. It changes what the Overview shows, not what the analyses do. Laboratory shows only laboratory signals, Infection prevention shows surveillance and integrated signals, Stewardship shows stewardship and integrated signals, and Executive shows everything but hides the Ask tab content, the data-quality table and the evidence graph. Your choice is remembered in the browser. It is disabled while signed in to a server, where your account role is used instead.


Dark mode


The sun/moon button in the header (title "Toggle dark/light mode") switches between light and dark themes.

Glossary




Access / Watch / Reserve (AWaRe)
The WHO AWaRe classification of antibiotics. Stewardship uses it to split use into three groups. The WHO reference target quoted in the app is at least 70% of use from Access. The list in the app is illustrative and should be checked against the current WHO AWaRe book.


Alert (Trends)
A series listed on Trends because at least two of the three detectors have flagged it and the dashboard interval check is met. It is a prompt to look, not a finding.


Antibiogram
A table of the percentage of isolates of each organism that are susceptible (or resistant) to each antibiotic, usually by ward or period. The "Resistance by ward (last 6 months)" table on Trends is of this kind, showing % resistant and n.


Audit trail
Session-only record of each analysis: time, module, method, output. Exportable as CSV. Not a versioned production log.


Baseline
Months 1 to 6 of a 24-month series. Its mean (and standard deviation, floored at 0.5 percentage points) is what the three detectors compare later months against.


CLSI
Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute, publisher of breakpoint tables (M100). Named in the app as a source of breakpoints that are not embedded.


Concordant signal
Two or three detectors agree. Status text on Trends is "Concordant signal (2 of 3 detectors)" or "(3 of 3 detectors)".


Count test (exploratory)
A comparison rule on Trends: at least 3 resistant isolates in the last 3 months and an exact one-sided binomial probability below 0.01 against the baseline proportion. It is evaluated and compared but is not used by the dashboard.


CUSUM
Cumulative sum detector. One-sided. See the Detectors section.


DDD
Defined daily dose, the unit of antimicrobial use in the Stewardship page's simulated data.


Dashboard rule
The rule that decides whether a series is listed on Trends: two of three detectors have signalled, plus at least 5 resistant isolates in the last 3 months, plus the lower 95% Wilson limit of the last 3 months' pooled proportion above the pooled proportion of months 1 to 6.


Decoy
In the "one planted rise and one decoy" scenario, a second series with one month in months 10 to 19 containing only 3 or 4 isolates, most of them resistant. It tests whether detectors are fooled by a tiny sample. An alert on it is a false alert.


Denominator
The number of isolates tested (n) that a percentage is based on. Reports and cards state it ("denominator/evidence"). Different analyses use different denominators and they are not interchangeable.


Detection delay
Months between a scenario's fixed onset month and a detector's first detection. A demonstration metric.


Drift watch
On Reliability: compares the share of flagged results in the first 30 events with the latest 20, per module; needs 50 events.


EUCAST
European Committee on Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing. The laboratory rules are "loosely informed by EUCAST expert-rule concepts"; its breakpoints are not embedded.


EWMA
Exponentially weighted moving average detector. See the Detectors section.


False alert
An alert in a series where nothing was planted in the synthetic data.


Ground truth (synthetic)
What the generator planted. For laboratory self-consistency it is simply the rule engine's own output.


I (intermediate)
See S/I/R.


Isolate
One cultured organism from a specimen, with its susceptibility results. Counts on Trends are isolates, not patients.


Line list
A table with one row per isolate (or per count) with dates, ward, organism and result. Trends works on a generated line list summarised by month.


Low numbers
A series with fewer than 30 isolates in the last 6 months. A warning of limited evidence (rule DQ-001); the data are not invalid. Ward cells with fewer than 20 isolates are shown in italics, and chart months with fewer than 10 isolates have open circles.


MDR
Multidrug-resistant. In the laboratory rules, three or more non-intrinsic resistances add "MDR features".


MIC
Minimum inhibitory concentration: the lowest antibiotic concentration that stops visible growth (broth microdilution). Result categories are derived from it by breakpoints. The app takes S/I/R results as input and does not compute MICs.


Moving average
Three-month running mean of the monthly percentage. See the Detectors section.


MRSA / VRE
Methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (cefoxitin used as surrogate) and vancomycin-resistant Enterococcus. Used as surveillance indicators and laboratory patterns; phenotype only.


Planted rise
A deliberate rise in resistance put into the synthetic data (for example K. pneumoniae with meropenem in the ICU rising from a 3% baseline to 30% over 4 months from month 17) so detectors can be scored.


Presumptive
Suggested but not confirmed by a reference method. All flags and identifications in the app are presumptive.


S / I / R
Susceptible, Intermediate (susceptible, increased exposure in current EUCAST wording) and Resistant: the three result categories for a tested antibiotic. "Not tested" is treated as missing, never as susceptible.


Series
One sequence of 24 monthly counts for an organism, antibiotic and ward (or all wards).


Session seed
The number that drives every random generator in the app. The same seed with the same click order reproduces the same results.


Signalling at end of window
Whether a detector is above its limit at the last month shown. The moving average always describes the last months; EWMA and CUSUM stay "ever signalled" after a crossing even if they later fall back.


Synthetic
Generated by the app from a random seed. No real patient or laboratory data.


Two-of-three rule
At least two of moving average, EWMA and CUSUM flagged. Rule SURV-001: two or more agree is high, one is moderate.


Wilson interval
A 95% confidence interval for a proportion, used on Trends charts and in the dashboard rule.









Detectors


All detectors work on a series of monthly percentages v[1..24] (resistant divided by tested, times 100; a month with no isolates counts as 0). They are the same on Surveillance, Trends, Validation and the real-counts check. Months below are counted from 1.


Baseline


mean = average of v[1..6]; sd = sqrt( sum((v[i]-mean)^2) / 6 ) (divisor 6); sd = max(0.5, sd).


Moving average


MA[i] = mean of the last up to 3 values ending at i. latest = mean(MA[22], MA[23], MA[24]). change% = (latest - mean) / mean * 100 (0 if mean is 0). The detector is flagged when change% >= 40. First-detection month is the first month where MA[i] > 1.4 * mean and MA[i-1] > 1.4 * mean. The flag always describes the last months, so "ever" and "now" are the same for it.


EWMA


alpha = 0.3; E[0] = mean; E[i] = alpha * v[i] + (1 - alpha) * E[i-1]. Upper limit: UCL = mean + 2.7 * sd * sqrt( alpha / (2 - alpha) ). Flagged (and first detection) at the second consecutive month with E[i] > UCL. Once flagged it stays flagged ("ever signalled"). "Signalling at end of window" means E[24] > UCL and E[23] > UCL.


CUSUM (one-sided, upward)


slack = 0.5 * sd; h = 4 * sd; S[0] = 0; S[i] = max(0, S[i-1] + v[i] - mean - slack). Flagged and first detected when S[i] > h for the first time. Stays flagged once crossed. "Signalling at end of window" means S[24] > h. A downward step is not flagged.


Concordance (rule SURV-001)


Surveillance page: two or three detectors flagged is high ("Concordant signal"); one is moderate ("Single-algorithm signal - monitor"); none is "No algorithm flagged a signal". Trends signal level is high only when all three flagged and the series is not low-numbers, otherwise moderate.


Dashboard rule (Trends alerts)


A series is listed when all of these hold:


    

  • At least 2 of the 3 detectors have flagged (ever).
    • 

  • Last-3-months pooled resistant count r3 >= 5.
    • 

  • Lower 95% Wilson limit of r3 / n3 is greater than the pooled proportion of months 1 to 6, r6 / n6 (taken as 0 if months 1 to 6 have no isolates).
    • 



Wilson interval


z = 1.96; p = k/n; d = 1 + z^2/n; c = p + z^2/(2n); a = z * sqrt( p(1-p)/n + z^2/(4n^2) ); interval = (c - a)/d to (c + a)/d, clipped to 0 and 1. For n = 0 the interval is 0 to 100%.


Count test (exploratory, compared not used)


p0 = max(0.01, r6/n6); flagged when r3 >= 3 and the exact one-sided binomial tail P(X >= r3 | n3, p0) < 0.01.


Low-number thresholds


    

  • Series low-numbers flag: fewer than 30 isolates in the last 6 months (rule DQ-001).
    • 

  • Ward table italic cells: fewer than 20 isolates in six months.
    • 

  • Chart open circles: fewer than 10 isolates in the month.
    • 



Stewardship trend (rule STEW-001 in the browser)


first = mean of months 1-6, last = mean of months 19-24, change% = (last - first)/first * 100. At least +60% is "High-priority utilisation increase"; at least +30% is "Rising utilisation - review"; at most -20% is "Decreasing utilisation". The server registry text gives +50% high and +20% moderate for last 3 months against the prior 3; the browser page uses the six-month comparison above.


Planted-signal check scoring


Evaluation is at the end of month 24 on series with baseline months 1 to 6. Six rules are scored: moving average, EWMA, CUSUM, 2 of 3 agree, 2 of 3 agree + interval check (the dashboard rule), and the count test. Delay is counted from the planted onset (month 17). Seeds: dataset i uses 7000 + 31*i with a planted rise and 7000 + 31*i + 1 without. The real-counts check uses seed 20261101 and plants a rise (+5, +10 or +20 percentage points, ramped over four months from window month 17, capped at 95%) into every sixth 24-month window.

What "synthetic" means here


All data are generated in the browser from the session seed (shown on Overview, Trends and Evidence). Isolate counts are Poisson-distributed by organism and ward volume, and resistant counts are binomial draws around a baseline resistant share with some random variation. Planted rises, decoys, outbreaks and testing changes are added by the generator, which is why the app can say which alerts are false. Synthetic data represent no real hospital, patient or laboratory, and results on them say nothing about performance on real data. Set a seed on Evidence to reproduce a session.

Evidence ladder
The ladder shows four levels of evidence and which have been reached. Level 1 means the logic follows published rules and the exact definitions in this Reference. Level 2 means it has been checked on synthetic data (regression tests and planted signals). Level 3, testing on independent real laboratory data, is not established. Level 4, evidence that use improves care, is not validated. Nothing here should be read as clinical or public-health evidence.
